History and utility sessions

A closed terminal is not gone. It moves to the project's history, where you can read it or pick the conversation up again.

History

Open it with ⌘ Y, or the clock on a project's row.

  • Search by terminal name, by the task it was given, or by its last reply.
  • Filter to this project or all projects, and to Claude or Codex.
  • Continue reopens the terminal and resumes the same conversation in the tool.
  • View transcript shows what was on the terminal.

Each project keeps up to 500 closed terminals. Transcripts are stored with secrets redacted, up to 2 MiB each.

An agent can use the history too: a terminal it closed with connecto close lands here like any other.

Utility sessions

With a dozen terminals open, claude-7 tells you nothing. Click the robot on a terminal's row and Connecto gives it a title of two to five words, taken from what the terminal is actually doing.

The title is written by a small, separate Claude session that Connecto starts out of sight. It has no tools and cannot touch your files or the other terminals. It reads the terminal's task, its last reply and its screen, with secrets redacted, and returns a title.

  • View → Utility Sessions lists the recent ones. Open session shows what the model was given and what it said.
  • The gear there sets the model (Haiku, Sonnet or Opus) and the effort. The default is Haiku at low effort, which is quick and costs little.

A title uses your Claude plan, like any other Claude session.
